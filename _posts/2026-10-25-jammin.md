For this project, I made my LilyPad Arduino play a song on its buzzer, with a switch that turns the music on and off. When the switch is on, the song plays. When the switch is off, the board goes quiet.

I picked "Happy Birthday" because it is short and easy to recognize. I used an Arduino buzzer tutorial on the Build Electronic Circuits website by Øyvind Nydal Dahl as a reference: [Arduino Buzzer Tutorial: Play Melodies with Your Arduino](https://www.build-electronic-circuits.com/arduino-buzzer/). I also used this [note frequency reference](https://pages.mtu.edu/~suits/notefreqs.html) for the musical notes.

The switch is one I had snapped off the ProtoSnap earlier, so I connected it back to the board with two alligator clips. One clip goes from the switch to the A9 tab, and the other goes from the switch to the negative (-) tab.

I had to make a few changes to get the song to repeat and respond to the switch. First, I put the music inside the `loop()` function so it would keep playing instead of stopping after one round. I also made the program play one note at a time instead of playing the whole song at once. This gave me a chance to check whether the switch was still on before playing the next note.

One thing I noticed was that the music doesn't stop immediately when I turn off the switch. Instead, it finishes the current note before stopping. When I turn the switch back on, the song starts again from the beginning. I also changed the buzzer pin to A3 to match my ProtoSnap board.

**Tip for my past self:** Get the switch working before you add any music. I tested the switch first with a simple light that turned on and off when I slid it, so I knew the clips and the board were fine. Then when I added the song, any problem had to be in the music code, and I wasn't hunting for a loose clip at the same time.
