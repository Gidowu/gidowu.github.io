For this project I made a light-up felt plushy. It has three LEDs, a button, a slide switch and a coin cell battery, and everything is connected with conductive thread instead of wires. The LEDs only turn on when the slide switch is ON **and** the button is pressed. If either one is off, the LEDs stay off.

### Step 1: Paper prototype

Before touching any fabric I planned the circuit on paper. I placed paper versions of the parts on the plushy template, labeled the + and − ends and drew pencil lines where the conductive thread would go. The three LEDs are connected in parallel: all the + sides are connected together and go to the button, and all the − sides are connected together and go to the slide switch.

![Paper prototype of the circuit plushy]({{ '/assets/img/plushy-paper-prototype.jpg' | relative_url }})

### Step 2: Testing with alligator clips

Next I put the real parts on the felt and connected them with alligator clips to check that the circuit worked before sewing anything. This is where I could test every button and switch combination and make sure the LEDs only lit up when both were on.

![Alligator clip prototype of the plushy]({{ '/assets/img/plushy-alligator.jpg' | relative_url }})

### Step 3: Sewing it

Once the circuit worked, I sewed it with conductive thread using a running stitch. At every metal hole on the parts I looped the thread through 3–5 times so it holds tight and makes good contact. I used separate pieces of thread for the + side and the − side so they never touch, because if they touch the circuit shorts out. I also stitched a star in embroidery floss for decoration. To finish, I folded the felt in half at the middle so the front and back line up, stuffed it and closed the edges with a whip stitch in regular (non-conductive) thread.

### One tip for someone doing this for the first time

Never let one piece of conductive thread touch both holes of the button or both holes of the slide switch. The thread is basically a wire, so if one thread goes into both holes it connects them and the button or switch gets skipped. Your LEDs will just stay on no matter what you press. Knot and cut the thread at the first hole and start a new thread at the second one.

Also, test with alligator clips first. It is much easier to move a clip than to pull out stitches.
