For this project, I made my LilyPad Arduino light up its LEDs in a random pattern. The board has four pins that can be dim or bright (the PWM pins: 6, 7, 8 and 10). Every second, the program picks one of them at random. If that pin is off, it turns on at a random brightness. If it is already on, it turns off. The other pins are left alone, so a few of them can be lit at the same time, each at its own brightness.

To keep track of everything, I used two lists (arrays). The first list holds the four pin numbers. The second list says whether each pin is on or off. The two lists line up by position so the first spot in the second list is the state of the first pin in the first list. This is easy to not get right, because pin 10 is not in spot 10. It is in spot 3, since the lists only have four spots and start counting at 0. I picked a random spot, not a random pin number, and then looked up the pin and its state from that spot.

Using lists also meant I did not have to write the same lines over and over for each pin. One loop sets up all four pins, and the rest of the program works for any pin that gets picked.

![My LilyPad lighting up at random](/assets/img/arrays-output.jpg)

In the photo, the red and blue LEDs are on, and the green LEDs are off right now.

**Tip for my past self:** Keep the two lists lined up, and always think "spot number" instead of "pin number". Pin 10 is not in spot 10, and that confused me at first.
