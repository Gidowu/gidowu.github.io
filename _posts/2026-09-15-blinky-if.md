For this deliverable, I wrote an Arduino program for my LilyPad USB Plus that practices using conditionals (`if` / `else if` statements) to control multiple LEDs based on timing.

The idea is that four LEDs — on pins 5, 6, 7 and 8 — take turns lighting up once per second based on which pin's "multiple" has been reached. So the pin 5 LED turns on after 5 seconds then at 6 seconds it hands off to the pin 6 LED, at 7 seconds to pin 7 and at 8 seconds to pin 8. Each LED stays lit until it's another pin's turn — so if none of the pins' conditions are true on a given second, the currently lit LED just keeps glowing until the next handoff happens. This repeats forever for as long as the board stays powered on.

**A tip for my past self:**

Before assuming your code is broken, physically check that the pin numbers in your code actually match where things are wired on your specific board — it's easy to lose an hour chasing a "bug" that's really just a mismatch between what you assumed and what's actually connected. Testing on the real hardware early (instead of only reading the code) saves a lot of guessing.
